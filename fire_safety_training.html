Why Fire Safety Matters

111K
Non-residential fires per year (NFPA)
$3.3B
Annual direct property loss from fires
2,900+
Civilian fire deaths annually in the U.S.

🔑 The good news: Most fires are entirely preventable. With proper training, you can identify risks early, respond quickly, and protect yourself and others. OSHA requires fire safety training for all employees under 29 CFR 1910.38 and 1910.157.

🏠 At home, fires kill more Americans than all natural disasters combined. A working smoke alarm cuts your risk of dying in a home fire in half.

Module 2

The Fire Triangle

  • 🌡️
    Heat — energy that ignites fuel. Sources include sparks, open flames, friction, and hot surfaces.
  • 🪵
    Fuel — any combustible material: wood, paper, fabrics, flammable liquids, gases, and electrical equipment.
  • 💨
    Oxygen — fire needs at least 16% oxygen. Normal air is ~21%, making most environments fire-ready.

💡 To extinguish a fire, remove at least one element: cool it with water, smother it with CO₂, or remove the fuel source.

Module 3

Classes of Fire

Understanding the type of fire is critical — using the wrong extinguisher can be dangerous or deadly.

A
Ordinary
Wood, paper, cloth, trash, plastics. The most common fire type.
B
Flammable
Gasoline, oil, grease, paint, solvents. Never use water.
C
Electrical
Energized wiring, fuse boxes, motors, and appliances.
D
Metal
Combustible metals: magnesium, titanium, sodium. Rare but extremely dangerous.
K
Kitchen
Cooking oils, fats, and greases in commercial kitchens.

⚠️ Never use water on Class B (spreads fire), Class C (electrocution risk), Class D (violent reaction), or Class K fires. Always match the extinguisher to the fire class.

Module 4

Using a Fire Extinguisher: The PASS Method

Remember the PASS method — four steps that could save your life.

P
Pull
Pull the safety pin to break the tamper seal. The extinguisher is now ready.
A
Aim
Aim the nozzle LOW — at the BASE of the fire, never at the flames.
S
Squeeze
Squeeze the handle slowly and evenly to release the agent.
S
Sweep
Sweep side to side at the base until the fire is fully out.

🚨 When NOT to fight the fire: If the fire is larger than a wastepaper basket, spreading rapidly, producing toxic smoke, or blocking your exit — evacuate immediately and call 911. No property is worth your life.

Module 5

Workplace Fire Hazards

  • ⚡
    Electrical hazards — overloaded outlets, frayed cords, damaged wiring, and equipment left running unattended.
  • 🧪
    Flammable substances — improperly stored chemicals, solvents, and cleaning agents near ignition sources.
  • 🔥
    Heat sources — space heaters too close to combustibles; unattended cooking in break rooms.
  • 📦
    Blocked exits — storage items obstructing fire exits, evacuation routes, or extinguisher access.
  • 🚬
    Improper smoking — smoking in unauthorized areas or improper disposal of cigarette materials.
  • 🗑️
    Waste accumulation — excessive paper waste or trash near ignition sources or electrical panels.
  • 🔧
    Hot work — welding, cutting, or grinding without proper fire watch protocols or hot work permits.
  • 🏭
    Combustible dust — fine particle accumulation in manufacturing, woodworking, or food processing areas.
Module 6

Home Fire Hazards

🍳
Kitchen
Unattended cooking is the #1 cause of home fires. Never leave the stove unsupervised. Keep flammables away from burners.
🕯️
Open Flames
Unattended candles, fireplaces, and fire pits. Always extinguish candles before leaving a room or going to sleep.
🔌
Electrical
Overloaded power strips, old wiring, extension cords under rugs. Have wiring inspected by a licensed electrician regularly.
🧺
Dryer Lint
Clogged dryer lint traps cause thousands of fires annually. Clean the lint trap after every single load.
🛋️
Smoking
Smoking materials are a leading cause of home fire deaths. Never smoke in bed or near flammable materials.
♨️
Heating
Space heaters need 3 feet of clearance on all sides. Keep combustibles away from furnaces and radiators.
Module 7

Fire Prevention Strategies

🏢 At Work
  • ✅Conduct regular fire risk assessments of your workspace
  • ✅Keep fire exits, extinguishers, and sprinklers unobstructed at all times
  • ✅Store flammable materials in properly labeled, approved containers
  • ✅Participate in fire drills at least once per year as required by OSHA
  • ✅Inspect and test smoke alarms and sprinkler systems on a regular schedule
🏠 At Home
  • ✅Install smoke alarms on every floor, inside every bedroom — test monthly
  • ✅Create and regularly practice a home escape plan with two exits per room
  • ✅Keep a fire extinguisher in the kitchen and know how to use it
  • ✅Replace smoke alarm batteries at least once a year (or use 10-year batteries)
  • ✅Never leave cooking, candles, or any open flame completely unattended
Module 8

Evacuation Procedures

1
Sound the alarm — activate the nearest fire alarm pull station immediately.
2
Call 911 — notify emergency services even if you think the fire is small.
3
Close doors — close (don't lock) doors as you leave to slow the spread of fire and smoke.
4
Use stairs only — never use elevators during a fire evacuation, ever.
5
Stay low — crawl if there is smoke; fresh, breathable air stays near the floor.
6
Assembly point — proceed to your designated meeting area. Do not re-enter the building.

🚪 Before opening any door — feel it with the back of your hand. If it's hot, DO NOT open it. Find an alternative exit or shelter in place.

🧯 If trapped — seal gaps under the door with clothing, signal from a window, and call 911 with your exact location.

👥 Account for everyone — your Fire Warden should perform a headcount at the assembly point and report any missing persons to first responders immediately.

Module 9

Emergency Response

🔥 If You Discover a Fire:
  • 1Stay calm — panic leads to poor decisions and puts others at risk
  • 2Alert others verbally and activate the nearest fire alarm
  • 3Only attempt to extinguish if trained, fire is small, and exit is clear
  • 4Evacuate immediately using the nearest safe, unobstructed exit
  • 5Call 911 from a safe location outside the building
🛑 Stop, Drop & Roll:
  • 🛑
    STOP — do not run if your clothing catches fire. Running fans the flames.
  • ⬇️
    DROP — drop immediately to the ground and cover your face with your hands.
  • 🔄
    ROLL — roll back and forth to smother the flames until they are completely out.

🚑 After evacuation, seek medical attention immediately for any burns or smoke inhalation — symptoms may not appear right away but can be life-threatening.

Module 10

OSHA Requirements

  • 📋
    Fire Prevention Plan 29 CFR 1910.38 — employers must maintain a written plan detailing fire hazards, control measures, and employee responsibilities.
  • 🧯
    Extinguisher Training 29 CFR 1910.157 — employees expected to use extinguishers must receive hands-on training at least annually.
  • 🚪
    Emergency Action Plan 29 CFR 1910.38 — must include evacuation procedures, alarm systems, and employee accountability procedures.
  • 🔍
    Regular Inspections — fire extinguishers must be inspected monthly and professionally serviced annually.
  • 🏃
    Exit Routes 29 CFR 1910.37 — exits must be clearly marked, unobstructed, and properly illuminated at all times.
  • 🔔
    Alarm Systems — fire detection and alarm systems must be tested and maintained on a regular schedule.

⚠️ Non-compliance with OSHA fire safety standards can result in significant fines, legal liability, and loss of life. Annual training is mandatory for all employees.

Summary

Key Takeaways

🔺
Know the Triangle
Fire needs heat, fuel, and oxygen. Remove any one element and you stop the fire. This is the foundation of all fire safety.
🧯
PASS the Test
Pull. Aim. Squeeze. Sweep. Know how to use a fire extinguisher before an emergency happens.
🚪
Have a Plan
Know your evacuation routes, assembly point, and Fire Warden. Practice drills at least once a year.
⚠️
Prevent First
Identify and address hazards before they become fires. Inspect equipment, store chemicals safely, keep exits clear.
📢
Alert & Evacuate
Sound the alarm first. Call 911. Evacuate everyone. Your safety is the absolute priority — never go back in.
📚
Stay Trained
Review fire safety annually. Consult OSHA, NFPA, Red Cross, and USFA for updated guidelines and resources.
