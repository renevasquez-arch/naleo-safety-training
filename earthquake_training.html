Why Earthquake Preparedness Matters

10,000
Earthquakes occur in California every year — most too small to feel, but many are not (USGS)
$20B+
Economic damage caused by the 1994 Northridge earthquake — one of the costliest in U.S. history
500+
Active fault lines in California, including major faults running directly beneath Los Angeles

⚠️ A major earthquake is not a matter of if — it's when. USGS scientists estimate a 60% probability of a magnitude 6.7 or greater earthquake striking the greater Bay Area within 30 years. The Puente Hills fault beneath downtown Los Angeles could produce a magnitude 7.5 event. Being prepared before it happens is the single most effective thing you can do.

Module 2

Drop, Cover, and Hold On

The three-step action endorsed by USGS, FEMA, and the Red Cross — memorize it and make it automatic.

1
DROP
🙇
Get to your hands and knees immediately. This prevents being knocked down and keeps you mobile to move with shelter if needed. If you are in bed, stay there and protect your head with a pillow.
2
COVER
🛡️
Get under a sturdy desk or table. If no desk is nearby, move against an interior wall and cover your head and neck with your arms. Stay away from windows, exterior walls, and heavy furniture that could fall.
3
HOLD ON
✊
Grip the desk or table leg firmly. If it slides, move with it. Remain in position until all shaking completely stops — even after it seems like it's over, don't move until you're certain.

❌ MYTH: "Stand in a doorway." This is an outdated and dangerous myth. Modern building codes make doorways no stronger than any other part of the structure, and you risk being struck by the door swinging violently. Drop, Cover, and Hold On is the correct action — every time.

Module 3

Understanding California's Seismic Risk

🗺️
Major California Fault Lines
The San Andreas Fault runs 800 miles through the state. The Hayward Fault runs through Oakland and Berkeley. The Puente Hills Fault lies beneath downtown Los Angeles. The Cascadia Subduction Zone poses a major tsunami risk along the Northern California coast.
📊
The Magnitude Scale
Each whole number = 10× more ground motion and ~32× more energy released.

M 3.0 — Felt by many
M 5.0 — Minor damage possible
M 6.0+ — Significant damage
M 7.0+ — Major destruction
M 8.0+ — Great earthquake
📅
Recent Major Events
1994 Northridge M6.7 — 57 deaths, $20B+ damage, woke up the region to preparedness.

2019 Ridgecrest M7.1 — Largest California quake in 20 years; widely felt across Southern CA.

Scientists warn a M7.8+ on the San Andreas is long overdue.

🔬 The "Big One" is a real and present risk. USGS models show that a full rupture of the southern San Andreas Fault could cause shaking across 7 counties, damage 300,000 buildings, and displace over 250,000 people in the Los Angeles region alone. Preparedness at the individual and workplace level significantly reduces casualties.

Module 4

Before: Securing Your Workspace

Most earthquake injuries are caused by falling and flying objects — not by the building itself. Securing your workspace now is the most effective prevention step you can take.

🔩 Secure These Items
  • 🗄️Anchor heavy filing cabinets and bookshelves to the wall with L-brackets
  • 🖥️Secure computer monitors and screens with restraining straps
  • 📦Move heavy objects to lower shelves; lighter items on top
  • 🖼️Use closed hooks for picture frames, clocks, and wall decorations
  • ☕Clear pathways near your desk and exits of trip hazards
  • 🧪Store hazardous materials in lower, latched cabinets
🧭 Know Your Office
  • 🚪Identify your two nearest emergency exits — walk the routes now
  • 🧯Locate the fire extinguishers on your floor
  • 📍Know your designated outdoor assembly point
  • 👷Know who your floor warden is and save their contact
  • 🔑Know where the gas shutoff is for your building (if applicable)
  • 🏥Know the nearest medical facility to your workplace
Module 5

Before: Building Your Emergency Kit

Build a 72-hour kit — enough supplies for 3 days. After a major earthquake, utilities, stores, and roads may be unavailable. Keep one kit at home and a smaller go-bag at work or in your car.

💧
Water
1 gallon per person per day for 3 days minimum
🥫
Food
3-day supply of non-perishable, easy-to-prepare items
🔦
Flashlight
+ extra batteries or a hand-crank model
🩹
First Aid Kit
Include any medications, glasses, and hearing aids
📻
Battery Radio
For NOAA weather alerts and emergency broadcasts
🔋
Power Bank
Keep fully charged to power your phone after an outage
📄
Documents
Copies of IDs, insurance, and bank info in a waterproof bag
💵
Emergency Cash
ATMs may be down; small bills are best
🧤
Gloves & Mask
Work gloves + N95 mask for handling debris
👟
Sturdy Shoes
Keep a pair under your desk or in your go-bag

🎒 Keep a small go-bag at your desk. Include water, a snack bar, a flashlight, and your medication. After a major earthquake, you may not be able to return home right away — having supplies on hand keeps you self-sufficient.

Module 6

During: At the Office

When shaking starts, you have seconds. These steps should be automatic — don't stop to think.

  1. DROP immediately to your hands and knees — don't wait to decide if the shaking is serious
  2. Get under your desk or a sturdy table; if none is nearby, face an interior wall and cover your head and neck with both arms
  3. Hold on to the desk leg firmly — if the furniture moves, move with it
  4. Stay away from windows, bookshelves, filing cabinets, and exterior walls
  5. Do NOT run for the exit while shaking continues — falling debris is the #1 cause of injury
  6. If caught in a hallway, drop and cover against an interior wall, protecting your head with both arms
  7. Wait for shaking to fully stop — wait several seconds after the last movement before changing position
  8. Once stable, check yourself and those nearby for injuries before beginning to evacuate

⚠️ Do not try to run outside during shaking. Most earthquake injuries happen when people try to move. Glass shatters, ceilings collapse, and objects fall — staying in place under cover is statistically the safest action.

Module 7

During: Special Situations

🏢
In a High-Rise Building
Expect significant swaying — modern high-rises are engineered to flex, not break. Drop, Cover, and Hold On well away from windows and exterior walls. Fire alarms and sprinklers may activate. Do NOT use the elevators. After shaking stops, evacuate via stairs, watching for debris and structural damage on each floor.
🚗
In a Vehicle
Pull over away from overpasses, bridges, power lines, and trees as quickly and safely as possible. Stop and stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt fastened until shaking stops. Tune in to emergency radio broadcasts. After shaking, proceed slowly — check for road damage, downed power lines, and bridge damage before crossing.
🌊
Outside / Near the Coast
Move away from buildings to avoid falling glass and debris. Get to open ground if possible. If you are near the coast, move immediately to higher ground after strong shaking — a major offshore earthquake can trigger a tsunami within minutes. Do not wait for an official warning. If you felt it, go.

📱 ShakeAlert may give you seconds of advance warning. If you feel your phone buzz with an emergency alert before shaking starts, Drop, Cover, and Hold On immediately — every second counts.

Module 8

After: Immediate Actions

Once shaking has completely stopped, take these steps in order.

✅ DO
  • 🩺Check yourself for injuries before standing up or moving
  • 🪜Use the stairs to evacuate — never the elevator
  • 📍Proceed to your designated outdoor assembly point
  • 👷Check in with your floor warden — all personnel must be accounted for
  • 👃Sniff for gas before turning on any electrical switches
  • 📻Listen to a battery-powered radio for emergency instructions
❌ DO NOT
  • 🚫Re-enter any building until cleared by emergency services
  • 🛗Use elevators — they may have damaged mechanisms or lose power suddenly
  • 🔥Use open flames — gas leaks may be present throughout the area
  • 📞Use phones except for genuine emergencies — keep lines open
  • 😌Assume it's over — significant aftershocks may follow soon
  • 🏃Move seriously injured people unless they are in immediate danger
Module 9

After: Hazards to Watch For

🔄
Aftershocks
Aftershocks can follow for days or weeks after a major quake. Some may be nearly as strong as the main event. Each time one occurs, Drop, Cover, and Hold On immediately. Do not return to a building during an aftershock sequence.
💨
Gas Leaks
If you smell gas or hear hissing, do not touch any electrical switch — a single spark can ignite accumulated gas. Leave the building immediately, leave the door open behind you, and call 911 from a safe distance outside.
🔥
Fire
Earthquakes frequently cause fires from ruptured gas lines, downed power lines, and damaged appliances. If you see or smell smoke, follow standard evacuation procedures and call 911. Do not attempt to fight a large fire yourself.
🏚️
Structural Damage
Look for cracks in walls, ceilings, or floors; jammed doors; sagging floors or rooflines. These indicate unsafe structural conditions. Evacuate immediately and do not re-enter until a structural engineer provides written clearance.
⚡
Downed Power Lines
Stay at least 30 feet away from any downed power line — the ground around it may be energized. Never attempt to move a power line. Call 911 and warn others away from the area. Keep pets and children away.
🌊
Coastal Tsunamis
A major offshore earthquake is a natural tsunami warning. If near the coast, move inland and uphill immediately — do not wait for a siren or official alert. Tsunami waves can arrive within minutes of a nearby quake.
Module 10

ShakeAlert & Staying Informed

📡
What is ShakeAlert?
ShakeAlert is the USGS-operated earthquake early warning system for the U.S. West Coast. It detects the fast-moving P-wave (which causes little damage) and sends an alert seconds before the slower, destructive S-wave arrives. Those seconds can mean the difference between getting under a desk or being knocked down in the open.
📱
How to Receive Alerts
Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) are sent automatically to all cell phones in the affected area — no app required. Just make sure your phone's Emergency Alerts are turned ON in your notification settings.

For additional advance warning, download the MyShake app (UC Berkeley) — it provides early alerts and maps.
📻
Staying Informed After
LA Emergency Management: 1-800-675-4357
USGS Earthquake Hazards: earthquake.usgs.gov
CA Governor's OES: caloes.ca.gov
FEMA: fema.gov/earthquake
Great CA ShakeOut: shakeout.org

Tune to a battery-powered NOAA radio for shelter-in-place instructions.

⏱️ ShakeAlert can provide up to 30–60 seconds of warning for areas far from the epicenter — but areas near the epicenter may receive little or no warning. This is exactly why Drop, Cover, and Hold On must be your automatic response. Don't stop to check your phone first.

Summary

Key Takeaways

🙇
Drop, Cover, Hold On
The single most important action during an earthquake. Practice it until it is automatic. Every second counts — do not stop to think about it when shaking starts.
🔩
Secure Your Workspace
Anchor heavy items before a quake strikes. Most injuries are caused by falling objects, not the building itself. A five-minute anchoring job today can prevent a serious injury.
🎒
Build Your 72-Hour Kit
Water, food, flashlight, first aid, radio, power bank, and documents. Keep one kit at home and a go-bag at work. When infrastructure fails, your kit keeps you self-sufficient.
🚪
Know Exits & Assembly Point
Know your two nearest exits and your outdoor assembly point before an emergency. Don't look for them while the building is shaking — walk the route today.
🚫
Never Re-Enter Early
Wait for official clearance before re-entering any building. Aftershocks are common and structural damage may not be visible. Let emergency services assess safety first.
📱
Enable ShakeAlert
Turn on Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phone. Check your notification settings today. Those extra seconds of warning can give you time to get under cover before the shaking hits.
