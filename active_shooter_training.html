🎯 Your role: You don't need to be a hero. You need to know three things: Run. Hide. Fight. This training will teach you exactly what each means and when to use it.

Module 2

Run. Hide. Fight.™

Developed by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Run. Hide. Fight. is the nationally recognized response framework — three options in order of preference.

🏃
RUN
Evacuate immediately if there is an accessible escape path. This is always your best option.
🚪
HIDE
If you cannot run safely, find a secure hiding place. Lock doors, silence your phone, stay out of sight.
✊
FIGHT
Only as a last resort. If your life is in imminent danger, act aggressively to incapacitate the shooter.

⚠️ Important: These options are not always sequential. Assess your situation and choose the safest option available to you right now. Conditions change rapidly.

Module 3

🏃 RUN — Evacuate First

Running is your best option when a safe escape path is available. Do not hesitate — every second counts.

1
Have an escape route in mind — Know the exits in your building before an incident occurs. Identify at least two routes from your workspace today.
2
Leave your belongings behind — Do not stop to gather your purse, laptop, phone, or personal items. Your life is more important than your belongings.
3
Help others escape if possible — but don't wait — Warn others as you go, but do not let their hesitation prevent your escape. You cannot help anyone if you become a victim too.
4
Keep your hands visible — When exiting, raise your hands with fingers spread so law enforcement can clearly see you are not a threat.

🚨 Do not pull the fire alarm to evacuate — this may cause others to walk directly into the danger zone. Warn people verbally and directly as you pass them.

Module 4

🚪 HIDE — Shelter in Place

If running is not safely possible, find a secure location to hide. Your hiding place should be out of the shooter's view and provide physical protection.

✓ DO

  • Lock and barricade the door with heavy furniture
  • Turn off lights to appear unoccupied
  • Silence your phone completely (vibrate is not silent)
  • Stay away from doors and windows
  • Get behind solid objects (desks, filing cabinets)
  • Text 911 if you cannot safely speak
  • Stay hidden until law enforcement gives all-clear

✗ DON'T

  • Hide in groups in open hallways or lobbies
  • Open the door for anyone — even someone claiming to be police
  • Yell, make noise, or use flashlights visible under the door
  • Stand near windows or glass walls
  • Use elevators to escape
  • Assume the fire alarm means it's safe to leave
  • Leave your hiding spot until officially cleared

💡 Barricade tip: Use everything available — desks, chairs, bookshelves, filing cabinets. Even a belt looped through a door closer arm can prevent entry.

Module 5

✊ FIGHT — Last Resort Only

Fighting is only appropriate when you have no other option and your life is in imminent danger. If you must fight, commit fully — hesitation can be fatal.

⚠️ Only use this option when: You cannot run, you cannot hide, and the shooter is about to engage you directly.

🎯
Act with Aggression
Commit fully to stopping the threat. Yell, throw objects, rush the shooter. Hesitation is dangerous — act decisively.
🔧
Use Improvised Weapons
Fire extinguishers, chairs, laptops, scissors, hot coffee — anything within reach can be used as a defensive weapon.
👥
Work as a Team
Multiple people acting together are far more effective. Distract, swarm, and disarm simultaneously. Coordinate with others.

✅ Research shows: Unarmed civilians successfully stopped active shooters in multiple documented incidents by acting decisively together. Your actions matter.

Module 6

📞 Calling 911

Call 911 as soon as it is safe to do so. The information you provide helps law enforcement respond faster and more effectively.

1

Your Location

Building name, floor, and specific area. "I'm on the 3rd floor, east conference room."

2

Number of Shooters

How many shooters you saw or heard. If unknown, say so clearly.

3

Physical Description

Clothing color, height, hair, and any distinguishing features of the shooter.

4

Weapons Description

Type of weapon if seen — handgun, rifle, etc. Number of weapons observed.

5

Last Known Location

Where you last saw or heard the shooter and which direction they were moving.

6

Number of Victims

How many people appear injured and their location if known.

💡 If you cannot speak: Text 911 (available in most jurisdictions). If you dial and cannot speak, leave the line open — dispatchers are trained to listen and trace your location.

Module 7

🚔 When Law Enforcement Arrives

The arrival of law enforcement can be chaotic and frightening. Officers are focused on neutralizing the threat first — knowing how to behave keeps you safe.

1
Keep your hands visible at all times — Raise your hands, spread your fingers, and keep them in plain view. Do not reach for anything — including your ID or phone.
2
Follow all instructions immediately — Do exactly what officers say without hesitation. Drop to the floor if instructed. Officers cannot distinguish victims from threats until the scene is secured.
3
Do not grab officers or block their path — Officers must move quickly to neutralize the threat. Let them pass — even if you are injured, seek help after the scene is secure.
4
Put down any weapon immediately — If you fought off the shooter and have their weapon, put it down the moment law enforcement enters. Do not be holding it when they arrive.
5
Wait for the official all-clear — Do not leave your hiding spot until law enforcement gives an explicit verbal all-clear. First officers on scene are focused on the threat, not evacuation.
Module 8

⚠️ Recognizing Warning Signs

Most active shooters do not "snap" without warning. Research shows that in most cases, someone observed concerning behavior beforehand. Knowing the signs can prevent an incident.

Verbal Threats

Direct or indirect threats of violence toward coworkers, supervisors, or the organization.

Sudden Behavioral Changes

Dramatic mood shifts, increased aggression, withdrawal from colleagues, or unpredictable outbursts.

Obsession with Violence

Fascination with past shootings, weapons, or violent ideologies. Glorifying or identifying with perpetrators.

Expression of Grievances

Strong feelings of being wronged or persecuted — especially with expressions of wanting revenge.

Giving Things Away

Unexpectedly giving away personal items or saying goodbye in a way that seems unusually final.

Increased Isolation

Withdrawing from social activities, friends, and coworkers. Declining participation in team activities.

💡 Important: No single warning sign means someone will become violent. But combinations of these behaviors — especially with a specific target or plan — warrant immediate reporting.

Module 9

👁 If You See Something, Say Something

Reporting concerns early is the most powerful tool for preventing workplace violence. You are not "getting someone in trouble" — you may be saving lives.

🗣️
Report to Management or HR
If you observe concerning behavior from a coworker, report it to your supervisor, HR, or safety officer immediately. You can do so anonymously.
🚨
Call 911 for Imminent Threats
If someone makes a specific, credible threat — "I'm going to bring a gun tomorrow" — call 911 immediately. Do not wait to see if it was a joke.
📝
Document What You Observed
Write down exactly what was said or done, when, where, and who was present. Specific details help HR and law enforcement assess the threat accurately.

⚠️ Common barrier: Many people hesitate to report because they fear being wrong. Research from the FBI shows that in most mass shooting cases, warning signs were observed by someone who did not report them in time.

🏛️ NALEO policy: All reports of concerning behavior are taken seriously, investigated promptly, and kept confidential. There is no penalty for reporting in good faith.

Module 10

🩹 After the Incident

Once the scene is secured by law enforcement, there are important steps to follow for reunification, medical care, and recovery.

1
Proceed to the assembly point — South End Parking Lot. Check in with your floor warden so all staff are accounted for.
2
Seek medical attention — Even if you feel uninjured, adrenaline can mask pain. Report any injuries to first responders. Nearest facility: Garfield Medical Center, (626) 573-2222.
3
Do not return to the building — Wait for law enforcement and building management to officially declare the building safe before re-entering for any reason.
4
Cooperate with investigators — Law enforcement will want witness statements. Your observations — no matter how small — may be critical to their investigation.
5
Seek mental health support — Trauma responses are completely normal. NALEO will provide access to Employee Assistance Program (EAP) counseling. Contact HR for available resources.
