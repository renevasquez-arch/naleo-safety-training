Module 2

Password Security

🔑 Strong Password Principles
  • ✅
    Use at least 12–16 characters. Length beats complexity — "correct-horse-battery-staple" is stronger than "P@ssw0rd!"
  • ✅
    Use a unique password for every account. If one site is breached, your other accounts stay protected.
  • ✅
    Use a password manager (Microsoft Edge's built-in manager, Bitwarden, or 1Password) to generate and store strong passwords.
  • ✅
    Change a password immediately if you suspect it has been exposed — not on a fixed schedule.
🚫 What to Avoid
  • ❌Using your name, birthday, pet's name, or "NALEO" in your password
  • ❌Reusing your work password for personal accounts (Netflix, Gmail, etc.)
  • ❌Writing passwords on sticky notes on or near your monitor
  • ❌Sharing your password with anyone — including IT staff (real IT never asks for your password)
  • ❌Using simple substitutions like "P@ssw0rd" — attackers check these automatically

🛡️ Pro tip: Use a passphrase — three or four random words strung together (e.g., "purple-lamp-river-desk"). These are long, memorable, and very hard to crack.

Module 3

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds a second layer of protection so that even if your password is stolen, attackers still can't get in.

1
Enter Password
Sign in with your username and password as usual.
2
Verify Identity
Approve a prompt in the Microsoft Authenticator app — or enter a one-time code.
3
Access Granted
You're in. An attacker with just your password cannot pass this step.
99.9%
Attacks Blocked
Microsoft reports MFA blocks over 99.9% of automated account-compromise attacks.

📱 How to enable MFA on your Office 365 account: Go to aka.ms/mfasetup and follow the prompts to set up the Microsoft Authenticator app on your phone. Takes less than 5 minutes and dramatically improves your account security.

⚠️ Never approve an MFA prompt you didn't initiate. If you receive an unexpected approval request, deny it and change your password immediately — someone has your password and is trying to log in.

Module 4

Recognizing Phishing Emails

Phishing emails are designed to trick you into clicking a link, downloading a file, or giving up your credentials. Learn the red flags.

📧
Suspicious Sender
The display name says "Microsoft" but the actual email is from a random Gmail or misspelled domain (e.g., micros0ft-support.com).
⏰
Urgency & Threats
"Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" Attackers create panic to stop you from thinking critically.
🔗
Suspicious Links
Hover over any link before clicking. If the actual URL doesn't match what's displayed, don't click it.
📎
Unexpected Attachments
Unsolicited .zip, .exe, .docm, or .xlsm files can contain malware. Verify with the sender via phone before opening.
💳
Requests for Info
Legitimate organizations (Microsoft, your bank, NALEO IT) will never ask for your password, SSN, or credit card via email.
✏️
Grammar & Branding
Poor spelling, odd phrasing, or mismatched logos. Sophisticated attacks may look perfect — don't rely on this alone.

✅ When in doubt, don't click. Contact the sender via a known phone number or a separate email — not by replying to the suspicious message.

Module 5

Safe Email Practices in Office 365

  • ✅
    Report phishing in Outlook: Use the built-in "Report" button (or Report Message add-in) on any suspicious email. This alerts Microsoft and your IT team.
  • ✅
    Verify unexpected requests: If a "colleague" emails asking you to transfer funds, buy gift cards, or share files — call them directly to confirm.
  • ✅
    Check email rules regularly: Go to Outlook Settings → Mail → Rules. If you see auto-forwarding rules you didn't create, report it immediately — attackers add these silently.
  • ✅
    Use the Preview Pane carefully: Avoid opening emails from unknown senders in full view. Mark as junk and delete.
  • ❌Auto-forwarding all NALEO email to a personal Gmail or Yahoo account
  • ❌Clicking "Unsubscribe" on suspected spam — this confirms your email is active
  • ❌Opening password-protected zip files from unknown senders "to see what's inside"
  • ❌Sending sensitive documents (donor lists, financials, SSNs) via regular email — use SharePoint or OneDrive links instead
  • ❌Logging into your Office 365 account from a link in an email — always go directly to office.com
Module 6

Safe Browsing & Link Safety

🔍
Hover Before You Click
Always hover over a hyperlink to see its real destination in your browser's status bar. If the URL looks odd or doesn't match, don't click.
🔒
Look for HTTPS
Websites handling login or payment info should start with https:// and show a padlock icon. HTTP without the "S" is unencrypted.
🧩
Browser Extensions
Only install browser extensions from trusted sources (Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add-ons). Malicious extensions can steal passwords and session tokens.
📥
Safe Downloads Only
Only download software from official websites. Never run .exe, .dmg, or .msi files from email attachments or unknown sites.
🌐
Shortened URLs
Bit.ly and similar links hide the real destination. Use a URL expander (e.g., checkshorturl.com) or avoid clicking entirely.
📵
Pop-up Warnings
"Your computer is infected! Call 1-800-XXX-XXXX" is always a scam. Close the tab. Never call the number or grant remote access.
Module 7

Data Protection & Safe File Sharing

📁 What Counts as Sensitive Data?
  • 👤Personally Identifiable Information (PII): names, addresses, SSNs, phone numbers
  • 💳Financial information: bank accounts, credit cards, donor payment data
  • 🗳️Constituent and voter data managed by NALEO programs
  • 📊Internal HR records, salaries, and personnel files
  • 📝Contracts, grant agreements, and legal documents
✅ How to Share Files Safely
  • ✅
    Use OneDrive or SharePoint links — share a secure link, not an email attachment. Recipients don't need a copy; you maintain control.
  • ✅
    Set link permissions carefully — choose "Specific people" over "Anyone with the link" for sensitive files.
  • ✅
    Set expiration dates on shared links for time-sensitive documents.
  • ❌Never email sensitive files to personal accounts (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.)
  • ❌Never upload NALEO files to personal cloud storage (personal Dropbox, Google Drive)

🗑️ Physical data matters too. Shred printed documents with sensitive information. Don't leave printed reports, mail, or forms visible on your desk when stepping away.

Module 8

Device & Account Security

  • 🔒
    Lock your screen when you step away. Use Windows + L (PC) or Control + Command + Q (Mac). Even a brief absence is an opportunity.
  • 🔄
    Keep software updated. Enable automatic updates for Windows/macOS, Office 365, and your browser. Most ransomware exploits unpatched vulnerabilities.
  • 📶
    Use a VPN when working remotely. Public Wi-Fi at cafés, airports, and hotels is unencrypted. A VPN encrypts your connection so others on the network can't spy on your traffic.
  • 📱
    Secure your phone. Use a PIN, fingerprint, or Face ID. Your phone receives MFA codes — if someone has it unlocked, they have your second factor.
  • ❌Leaving your laptop unattended and unlocked in public spaces
  • ❌Plugging unknown USB drives into your work computer — "USB drops" are a known attack method
  • ❌Using personal, unmanaged devices to access NALEO files without checking with IT first
  • ❌Ignoring software update prompts — "I'll do it later" often means never
  • ❌Connecting to public Wi-Fi without a VPN to access work email or sensitive files
Module 9

Social Engineering & Impersonation

Social engineering bypasses technology entirely — it targets you. Attackers impersonate trusted people to manipulate you into taking an action.

📞
Vishing (Voice Phishing)
A caller claims to be from Microsoft IT Support, your bank, or the IRS — asking for passwords, remote access, or payment. Hang up. Real IT never calls and asks for your password.
💼
CEO / Executive Fraud
An email that appears to come from your CEO or a senior leader asks for an urgent wire transfer or gift card purchase. Always verify financial requests via a direct phone call.
🎭
Pretexting
An attacker creates a fake but plausible scenario ("I'm from HR and need your SSN to update payroll records"). Legitimate requests go through established processes — not cold calls.

🚩 Trust but verify — always out of band. If you get an unexpected request (even from a known colleague), hang up and call them back on a number you already have. Do not use any contact info provided in the suspicious message.

🧠 It's okay to say "I need to verify this first." Legitimate requesters will understand. Attackers will pressure you to act immediately — that pressure itself is a red flag.

Module 10

What To Do If You're Compromised

🖱️ If You Clicked a Suspicious Link:
1
Don't panic. Acting fast and calmly is more important than acting perfectly.
2
Disconnect from Wi-Fi / ethernet to prevent malware from spreading or communicating out.
3
Call NALEO IT immediately — do not try to fix it yourself. See the contacts slide for the number.
4
Change your passwords from a different, clean device after IT gives the all-clear.
🔑 If Your Password Was Stolen:
  • 1Change the compromised password immediately on a trusted device
  • 2Change passwords on any other account where you reused that same password
  • 3Check your Outlook inbox rules and forwarding settings for unauthorized changes
  • 4Review recent sign-in activity in your Microsoft account (myaccount.microsoft.com → Security)
  • 5Notify NALEO IT and HR so they can monitor for suspicious activity

🙋 Reporting an incident is not shameful. Cyber incidents happen to experts at the best-protected organizations in the world. Reporting quickly limits the damage. Silence is what makes incidents catastrophic.

Summary

Key Takeaways

🔑
Strong, Unique Passwords
Use 12+ characters and never reuse passwords. A password manager makes this easy — you only need to remember one master password.
📱
Always Use MFA
Enable MFA on your Office 365 account today. It blocks over 99.9% of automated attacks — even if your password is stolen.
🎣
Think Before You Click
Hover over links, verify senders, and treat urgency as a red flag. When in doubt, call the person directly using a known number.
📁
Share Safely
Use OneDrive and SharePoint links — not email attachments — for sensitive NALEO files. Never send PII or financials via personal email.
🔒
Secure Your Devices
Lock your screen, keep software updated, use a VPN on public Wi-Fi, and secure your phone with a strong PIN or biometrics.
📢
Report Immediately
If something looks wrong, contact NALEO IT right away. Early reporting saves data, money, and reputations. There is no shame in reporting.
